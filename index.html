Dates, geography and sources

Lines summarize historical transmission and exchange over real geography; their curves are not reconstructed travel itineraries. Modern borders orient the map. Each pin is a city or regional anchor, not a claim that every work listed there was composed at that exact spot.

c. means approximately. Century dates are shown as rounded year ranges. Dates on routes concern transmission. The timeline is a cumulative overview, not a reconstruction of which routes remained active in any year. Broad ranges appear at their earliest stated date; this is a display convention, not a claim to a precise foundation year. Pins can appear through an early exchange route before their later manuscript or printing milestone. Popups retain the full history and reading list, including later works. Each text has a separate formation date; manuscript and printing dates are identified as such. Most ancient dates are broad scholarly estimates. The traditional Mahinda mission is shown as a dashed line because its narrative is known from later chronicles.

This curated survey selects influential scriptures, treatises, regional authors and manuscript witnesses. It does not inventory every Buddhist text, school, version or route. Texts and transmission reviewed 20 October 2026. Geometry: Natural Earth, via world-atlas 2.0.2. Historical sources appear in the popups.
